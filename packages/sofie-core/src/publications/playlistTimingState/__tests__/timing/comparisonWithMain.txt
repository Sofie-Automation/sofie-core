The timing publication against main.

Written by comparisonWithMain.test.ts, next to this file. Do not edit it by hand: to rewrite it from what the code
gives today, run that test with UPDATE_TIMING_COMPARISON=1 set, and review the diff.

Each block is one moment of one show. Each row is a value a screen or the gateway shows: as main showed it, as it is
shown from the publication, and whether they are the same. A row says which timing modes it covers when it does not
cover all twelve; "the back modes" are back end, back end+duration and back start+end, and likewise for the none,
forward and duration modes. Lengths are m:ss, clock times are UTC, and Over/Under is O for over and U for under, as
the header shows it. "Shown by neither" lists what no screen shows and the gateway does not send at that moment.

The screens: Plan. Start to Seg. Budg. are the values of the rundown header. Segment durations are what the segment
headers show, fixed segment durations what they show in a view layout with a fixed segment duration, and segments
counting up what the shelf's segment timing panel shows when it counts up. On Air In are the countdowns to the first
part of each segment, and part countdowns those to the other parts. The gateway: part timing is activePlaylist's
currentPart.timing (startTime, expectedDurationMs, projectedEndTime), segment timing its currentSegment.timing
(expectedDurationMs, projectedEndTime), segments each segment's expectedDurationMs in the segments topic, and
playlist timers activePlaylist's timing.

The planned values and the gateway's playlist timers do not change during a show: every moment compares them, but
the table lists a row of theirs only the first time it reads as it does.

What differs from main:
- BackTime after deactivation: Once a BackTime playlist has been deactivated, main measured it against its planned
  end as if the whole show were starting now, so its over/under kept moving with the clock. The publication compares
  the duration as played with the planned duration, as it does after deactivation in every mode but forward
  start+end, which measures against its end time. After deactivation the duration as played is the planned length of
  the parts, so here it holds at U 0:00, however the show went.
- a take in multi-gateway mode: In multi-gateway mode a part starts a moment after its take (2 s here). In that
  moment main counted the part at its length, so its remaining duration held until the start. The calculator now
  counts the part to its end from its planned start, so that the projected end holds, and the remaining duration
  reads longer by the time left until the start. The estimated end and over/under therefore hold at what they read
  when the part starts, at the first take too, where main's moved on with the clock until then. They agree again at
  the start.
- an auto-next start not yet reported: In multi-gateway mode core schedules the start of the part that a part
  auto-nexts into, here B2 at 12:00:57, and B1 stays on air until the gateway reports that start, here a second
  later. In that second the calculator counts B2 as started but not on air: it leaves B2 out of the remaining
  duration, so the estimated end reads 20 s early and the over/under 20 s further under; its countdowns take the end
  of B2 for its start, so the parts after B2 read 20 s late; and it counts the time since B2's start twice in B, for
  B1 and for B2. Main calculated on every tick, and showed this for the whole second. The publication holds the
  documents of its last calculation, here at the report of B1's start, in which B1 runs over: the remaining duration
  holds at 1:40, the estimated end moves on with the clock, and the countdowns hold at B2 due now. When the
  publication calculates again within the second, as it does when anything else it reads changes, it shows what main
  showed, but the time in B then counts once from that calculation, and the gateway's
  currentSegment.projectedEndTime reads early by the time B2 had run by then. They agree again once the gateway
  reports the start.
- resolved lengths on the gateway: The gateway now sends a part's length as the calculator resolves it: a part with
  no length of its own in a display-duration group reads its share of the group, what the parts before it leave, and
  a segment's expectedDurationMs adds up these lengths. So in the segments topic it changes during the show: C reads
  0:40 until C1 is cut short at 7 s, and 0:43 after, with C2's share of 0:03. Main's gateway sent the length set on
  the part, 0:00, and C read 0:40 throughout.
- a part taken again, on the gateway: A part set as next again counts twice in its segment, in the segment header on
  main and on the publication alike. The gateway's currentSegment.projectedEndTime now comes from the same remaining
  time, so it counts the part twice too; main's gateway counted it once.
- new on the gateway: The gateway now sends the playlist timers the header reads, remainingDuration, estimatedEnd
  and timeInHand, as timer states for a consumer to evaluate. Main's gateway sent none.
